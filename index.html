🔍 Key Contributions

  • A joint rank-bit optimizer with optimality conditions: C1 selects each head's (r, b) optimum by balancing truncation loss against quantization loss, and C2 redistributes the total budget across heads by equalizing marginal distortion. Both are gradient-free, run once at calibration time, and add no per-token overhead.
  • A query-aware upgrade via attention-KL reordering: A second-order bound on the attention-KL divergence yields a per-direction importance weight wi = σ2Q,i σ2K,i. Reordering the SVD basis by this weight before C1/C2 makes the solver optimize attention quality instead of reconstruction MSE.
  • Graceful sub-1-bpd compression with no new kernel: KV-COBRA reuses the standard rotate-and-quantize kernel and stays accurate down to 0.5 bits per dimension where uniform-allocation baselines collapse. The same allocator extends to joint K+V compression.

Abstract

What limits KV-cache compression at extreme bit-rates? We argue that it is not the choice of compression scheme, but how its budget is allocated across attention heads. Existing methods apply rank and bit-width uniformly, ignoring that each head has a different optimal mix of rank truncation and quantization. We show that co-optimizing rank and bit-width per head—using only standard low-rank projection and scalar quantization—dominates uniform allocation, with the largest gains at low bit-rates. Our method, KV-COBRA (Co-Optimized Bit-Rank Allocation), formalizes this as a resource-allocation problem: it balances rank-truncation loss against quantization loss within each head, then redistributes budget across heads to minimize total distortion. A fused Hadamard rotation equalizes per-channel variance, and reordering the SVD basis by attention-KL importance makes the solver query-aware. The same allocator extends to joint K+V compression. On perplexity, zero-shot, and long-context benchmarks from 0.5 to 4 bits per dimension (bpd), KV-COBRA shows the smallest accuracy degradation among evaluated methods at low bpd, with no per-token overhead.

Problem: One (r, b) Does Not Fit All Heads

Generating each token with a large language model requires loading the entire key-value (KV) cache. Under long contexts the cache exceeds the model weights in size, so decoding becomes memory-bandwidth-bound. Per-token compressors keep every token but store it at fewer bits per dimension, and the strongest of them apply a top-r SVD truncation followed by b-bit quantization of the retained coordinates.

In these methods the rank r and the bit-width b are global hyperparameters applied uniformly to all attention heads. Yet heads differ dramatically in how their cache spectrum concentrates: under a uniform 2-bpd budget the per-head distortion spans more than 100× across the 256 KV heads of LLaMA-3.1-8B. A single r is wasteful for concentrated heads and inadequate for diffuse ones, and the same holds for a single b. The two choices are also coupled: the best rank for a head depends on its bit-width and vice versa.

KV-COBRA treats this as a classical rate-distortion allocation problem. Each head has a distortion curve over its rate, jointly over r and b, and the global task is to distribute the total rate across heads so that the sum of distortions is minimized. The resulting first-order conditions give a per-head rule for splitting the rate between rank and bits and a marginal-distortion-equalizing schedule across heads, both solvable in a few iterations at calibration time.

KV-COBRA Framework

Distortion model: Keeping the top-r SVD directions of a head discards the tail of its spectrum, and quantizing the retained coordinates at b bits adds a Bennett-type term proportional to 2−2b. The per-head distortion is D(r, b) = ∑i>r wi + q(b) ∑i≤r wi, with wi the key eigenvalue for the MSE objective or the query–key variance product for the attention-KL objective.

C1 — per-head rank and bit-width together: Given a head's budget B, C1 enumerates the even rank grid, sets b = B/r, and keeps the (r, b) with the smallest distortion. At the optimum the projection-loss gain from two more directions matches the quantization-loss cost on the retained ones. This is an O(d/2) search per head, run once.

C2 — cross-head redistribution: Using C1 as a black-box oracle D*(B), C2 moves budget between heads until their marginal distortions equalize, the water-filling condition of rate-distortion theory. A damped proportional update converges within five rounds on every tested model, and an exact dynamic-programming solver reaches the same perplexity within noise at 25–50× the cost.

Per-channel equalization via Hadamard rotation: The retained SVD coordinates have variances that decay by orders of magnitude. A Walsh–Hadamard rotation with a fixed random sign flip is fused into the projection matrix, so at zero latency every channel has the same expected variance and a single uniform b-bit quantizer per head suffices.

Attention-KL reordering (KV-COBRAKL): Downstream quality depends on the attention output, not on reconstruction error. A second-order expansion of the softmax KL divergence shows that the right weight for a direction is σ2Q,i σ2K,i: a direction with a small key eigenvalue may still be heavily attended. Reordering the SVD basis by this weight and plugging it into C1/C2 makes the whole allocation query-aware. The complete C1+C2 calibration takes under 200 ms on 7–8B models.

Experimental Results

We evaluate KV-COBRA on LLaMA-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct against KIVI, KVQuant, GEAR, TurboQuant, SVDq, and KQ-SVD, sweeping 0.5 to 4 bits per dimension on WikiText-2 / PTB / C4 perplexity, five zero-shot reasoning tasks, and five LongBench tasks. At 2 bpd and above the choice of allocator barely matters. At low bit rates KV-COBRAKL degrades most gracefully: integer-only quantizers cannot enter the regime, uniform-budget SVD baselines degrade sharply, and at a nominal 1-bpd budget KV-COBRAKL nearly doubles the LongBench F1 of the integer-only baselines.

The per-head (r*, b*) optima selected by C1 spread over many operating points and shift with the budget and the model, which is exactly why a single fixed (r, b) cannot serve every head. The MSE variant wins on perplexity, its direct objective, while the KL variant wins on LongBench and matches on zero-shot, the split predicted by the two objectives.

The same allocator extends to joint K+V compression at a fixed total budget, where V-heavy splits dominate on perplexity and LongBench, reflecting the flatter spectrum of values. The pattern also carries over to LLaMA-3.1-70B, Qwen2.5-72B, and Mixtral-8x7B, where KV-COBRAKL stays closest to FP16 on every metric.

Speedup of the per-layer attention primitive over FP16 scaled-dot-product attention (Hq = 32, d = 128, CUDA-graph captured, RTX 6000 Ada). KV-COBRA stores the cache as packed b-bit codes with the Hadamard rotation folded into the basis, so dequantization and the inverse rotation run in one fused pass. At the retained rank used in the paper (r = 16) the primitive is 1.79–1.89× faster than FP16 across 32k–128k tokens, and the gain holds up to r = 64.

Everything KV-COBRA adds happens once at calibration time. The C1+C2 solver runs in under 200 ms on the 7–8B models, and the complete one-time calibration of LLaMA-3.1-70B, including the calibration forward passes and the eigendecomposition of all 640 KV heads, takes 18.3 s. At inference there is no per-token overhead beyond the standard rotate-and-quantize kernel shared with prior work.
